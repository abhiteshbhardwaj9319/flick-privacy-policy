# Flick Privacy Policy

_Last updated: October 2026_

## What This App Does

Flick helps you look back at your photos (your timeline, photos from this day in past years, and albums), make collages and cards from them, and clear the ones you no longer need: duplicates, forwarded media, old screenshots and other files. All scanning, sorting, making and clearing happens entirely on your device.

## Data Collection

Flick does NOT collect, transmit, or store any of your files or file contents on external servers. Specifically:

- Your files are never uploaded anywhere
- No file contents are analyzed in the cloud
- No personal information is collected by Flick itself
- No analytics on which files you keep or delete

The Google AdMob and Google Play Billing services described below collect some data (such as an advertising ID, or payment details handled by Google) only when you choose to watch an ad or make a purchase. Google ML Kit, which lets Pip notice what is in photos and helps frame faces in collages on your phone, sends Google anonymous technical information, never photos. Flick downloads new collage designs and quotes from a public website and sends nothing about you when it does. See the sections below for details.

## Photos, Videos and Audio

Flick asks for access to your photos, videos and audio (on Android 14 and later you can share only some photos). It uses this to:

- Find your photos from the camera, screenshots, downloads and WhatsApp
- Show photos from this day, your timeline and albums
- Find duplicate copies by comparing files on your device

Flick does not ask for access to all your files. Other files (such as PDFs or APKs) are only touched when you choose them yourself in Android's file picker. None of this leaves your device.

Albums you make are stored only on your phone. If you turn on "Show in Gallery" for an album, Flick moves its photos into a Pictures/Flick folder on your phone, after you confirm.

## Looking for New Photos in the Background

To keep your library up to date, Flick may look for new photos in the background, at most about twice a day and only when your battery and storage are not low. This happens entirely on your phone; nothing is sent anywhere.

## Trash / Recycle Bin

When you clear photos, videos or audio, they are not deleted right away. On Android 11 and later they go to Android's own trash after you confirm (once per batch); on older Android they go to a private trash folder in Flick. Either way you can restore them from Recently cleared for 30 days. With Flick Premium, Flick keeps a private copy for 90 days. Files you choose in Android's file picker are deleted right away, after you confirm.

## Collages and Lines on Photos

The collage maker and "Add a line" work on your phone. To pick photos for a collage, Flick looks at how sharp and bright they are and where faces are, so a crop never cuts someone off. Face detection (Google ML Kit, through Google Play services) only finds where faces are in a photo; it does not recognise who anyone is. Those face positions are kept in Flick's private storage so it does not have to look again, and they never leave your phone.

Photos you choose with Android's photo picker (which can include Google Photos on some phones) are copied into Flick's private cache to make the collage. A collage is saved to Pictures/Flick/Collages on your phone only when you tap Save. Collages you save or share are also kept in "Your collages" in Flick's private storage (your favourites and the 20 newest) until you delete them.

## New Designs and Quotes

At most once a day, Flick checks a public folder on GitHub Pages (abhiteshbhardwaj9319.github.io) for new collage designs and quotes, and downloads any new files. It sends nothing about you: no account, no ID, nothing about your phone or your photos. Like any website, GitHub sees your phone's IP address when a file is downloaded. Flick works fully without this: the designs and quotes that come with the app are always there.

## Your Wrapped Card

"Your Wrapped" makes a card from your own photos on your phone. Your numbers (space freed, photos sorted, streak) appear on it only if you turn them on. When you share a Wrapped card, Flick writes the date in its private storage to open Pip's extra looks for 7 days; nothing is sent.

## Advertising

Flick shows an ad only when you choose to watch one (for example, to double your points after a session). Ads are provided by Google AdMob, which may collect device information and advertising identifiers as described in Google's Privacy Policy. Premium users never see ads. Flick does not use ad data for anything else.

## In-App Purchases

Flick Premium is a 1-year pass bought through Google Play's billing system. It does not renew by itself. Google handles your payment details directly; Flick does not receive, collect, or store them. See Google's privacy policy at https://policies.google.com/privacy for details.

## Ratings

Now and then, after a good cleanup, Flick may ask Google Play to show its rating card. Google shows it (or not) and handles your rating; Flick never sees who rated or what.

## Sharing

Sharing only happens when you tap Share: Flick creates the image on your phone and Android's share sheet sends it to the app you pick. When the Flick mark is on, the caption includes a link to Flick on Google Play. The link only says which part of Flick it came from (for example a collage or a Wrapped card), so Google Play can count installs from shares; it says nothing about you.

## Pip Noticing Photos

Pip can recognise what is in a photo (for example food, a dog or a beach) using Google ML Kit, which runs on your phone. Photos and what Pip recognises never leave your phone and are not saved. ML Kit sends Google anonymous technical information to keep it working well (device model, Android and app version, an install ID, speed and error codes); never photos. You can turn this off in Settings: "Pip notices what is in photos".

## Smart Groups

To find look-alike, blurry and dark photos, documents and photos of screens, Flick looks at each photo once, on your phone, and keeps a small summary in its private storage so it does not have to look again: a fingerprint number, how sharp and how bright the photo is, and whether it looks like a document or a screen. The summary never leaves your phone and is removed when you uninstall Flick.

## Notifications

If you allow notifications, Flick can send one notification a day built from your own photos (for example, photos from this date in past years) and, rarely, a low-storage alert. They are created on your device and nothing is sent to a server. If you turn on "Show a photo", a small copy of that photo is kept in Flick's private storage to show in the notification. You can change the time, turn previews off, or turn notifications off in Flick's Settings or your device settings.

## Third-Party Services

The app uses the following third-party services:

- Google AdMob — for displaying advertisements (collects advertising ID, crash/diagnostics, ad interaction data)
- Google Play Billing — for the Flick Premium 1-year pass (collects payment details directly; Flick never sees them)
- Google ML Kit — for Pip noticing what is in photos and for finding faces in collages, on your phone (sends anonymous technical information such as device model, app version, an install ID, speed and errors; never photos)
- GitHub Pages — hosts the public collage designs and quotes Flick downloads (sees the IP address of the download, like any website)

Each service has its own privacy policy governing data collection.

## Data Security

All app data (scan results, settings, progress, collages, and the feelings you tag photos with) is stored only on your device, in Flick's private storage, which Android keeps separate from other apps. Flick never sends this data to any server.
